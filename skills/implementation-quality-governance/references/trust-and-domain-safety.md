# Trust And Domain Safety

For repository-controlled execution, untrusted input, authorization, secrets, sensitive data, tenant boundaries, external systems, user-controlled output, or a security control, map each source to its sink and apply the relevant controls.

## Execution And Input Boundaries

- Before running a changed or untrusted repository-controlled check, inspect its entrypoint and transitive scripts, plugins, hooks, configuration, and invoked tools even when the top-level command is familiar. Use a non-executing preflight first. Run only after understanding effects, in containment without unrelated credentials, production access, or unnecessary filesystem/network privileges; obtain authorization for required elevated capability.
- Validate shape, type, encoding, size, count, depth, and resource/time limits at boundaries. Reject ambiguous or malformed input predictably; encode output and use parameterized data access.
- Canonicalize and contain file paths; use argument-array process APIs, safe parsers, and no unsafe deserialization. Verify webhook authenticity and replay protection. For uploads/archives, independently verify type, isolate storage, defend traversal/archive bombs, and bound processing.
- For SSRF, allowlist required schemes, hosts, ports, and destinations; reject ambiguous URLs, credentials, fragments, unsupported encodings, and special-use/private/link-local/loopback/metadata ranges. Canonicalize before policy checks, constrain redirects, resolve DNS safely, re-resolve at connection time where needed, enforce policy on every resolved address and redirect hop, and defend rebinding or resolution changes.

## Identity, Authorization, And Cryptography

- Where applicable, validate authentication and session lifecycle: parse credentials and tokens strictly; allowlist trusted algorithms and signing-key sources; verify token purpose/type, issuer, audience, signature, and key; enforce expiry, renewal, rotation, revocation, replay resistance, concurrent-use policy, and fail-closed behavior. For OIDC, validate state and nonce and require PKCE where applicable. Set appropriate Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite, scope, and lifetime cookie attributes; rotate sessions after authentication and privilege changes; invalidate them on logout. For recovery, MFA, and re-authentication or step-up, apply appropriate identity proofing, rate limits, one-time/expiry/replay controls, factor independence, and recent-authentication requirements; test bypass and downgrade. Add negative cases for missing, malformed, expired, revoked, replayed, invalid-signature, wrong-key, wrong-purpose/type, and wrong-issuer/audience credentials, plus fixation and lifecycle failures.
- Enforce deny-by-default server-side authorization from authenticated subject, action, resource ownership, and server-derived tenant context. Never trust caller-supplied tenant or owner identifiers for scope. For every changed authorization surface, add relevant negative tests across each applicable API, background-job, CLI/admin, and other enforcement path for cross-user, cross-role, cross-tenant, object/action, field-level, batch and indirect-reference substitution, ownership changes, and enumeration.
- Use approved cryptographic libraries and protocols, AEAD where confidentiality and integrity are required, a CSPRNG for keys/tokens/nonces, and an approved password KDF for passwords. Verify certificates and hostnames. Define key generation, storage, access, rotation, revocation, expiry, backup/recovery, and destruction; do not invent cryptography or reuse nonces unsafely.
- Preserve safe established CSRF, CORS, CSP, session, encryption, and header conventions. Do not expand privilege or choose insecure defaults.

## Secrets, Privacy, And Telemetry

- Use approved secret storage and least-privilege credentials. Never expose secrets in source, fixtures, prompts, bundles, artifacts, logs, or telemetry. Secret-scan the entire task-relevant diff/history including deleted lines, command output, logs, generated files, and intermediate artifacts. Any intermediate exposure requires immediate containment plus assessment of rotation/revocation and history/artifact remediation; deletion from the final diff alone is insufficient.
- For changed telemetry, inventory and allowlist fields; classify sensitivity and linkability; test redaction, truncation, and bounded cardinality; define access, retention/deletion, processors, destinations, and transfer controls. Do not expose sensitive, enumerable, tenant-revealing, or unauthorized identifiers publicly. Approved opaque operational identifiers may appear in access-controlled, privacy-safe telemetry when necessary for correlation. Never expose tokens, stack traces, or sensitive fields.
- For new or changed personal/sensitive-data flows, assess necessity and minimization, classification, destinations/processors, encryption, access/audit controls, retention/deletion, backups, residency/consent, and export/deletion workflows. Escalate legal/compliance obligations not established locally.

## High-Consequence Domains

Identify domain-specific invariants, authorization boundaries, reconciliation/audit requirements, applicable regulations, and failure containment before implementation. For payments or financial value flows, explicitly determine ledger/source of truth and reconciliation, payout/refund authorization limits and segregation of duties, idempotency and duplicate charge/refund handling, PCI scope, and applicable KYC/AML or regulatory obligations; escalate requirements not established locally.
