# Grill a Plan

Expose and resolve the decisions that determine whether a proposed plan, decision, or idea is sound. Be candid and constructive. The user owns the decisions, values, and tradeoffs; recommend a path, but do not decide for them.

## Establish the subject

- Restate the proposal, intended outcome, known constraints, and requested level of challenge. Use the user's existing context; do not make them repeat it.
- Inspect authorized local context or use available tools to establish facts. Distinguish verified facts, assumptions, and unknowns. Do not ask the user for a fact that can be found safely.
- Identify decision dependencies and start with the most consequential unresolved decision. If the proposal is too vague to evaluate, ask for the intended outcome first.

## Interview loop

Ask exactly one decision question per message and wait for the user's answer before continuing. Do not bundle a checklist or disguise several independent decisions as one question.

For every question:

1. Name the decision and why it matters now.
2. Give a recommended answer with a brief reason, plus a materially viable alternative when one exists.
3. Ask the user to choose, modify, or reject the recommendation.
4. Record the answer, its rationale, and any assumption or dependency it creates.

Challenge the areas that apply, rather than mechanically covering a fixed list: outcome and success measures; evidence; stakeholders; scope and non-goals; constraints; assumptions and unknowns; alternatives and opportunity cost; risks, failure modes, reversibility, and recovery; security, privacy, legal, or ethical effects; ownership, timing, resources, and dependencies; and measurement or stopping criteria.

When an answer changes an earlier decision, revisit the affected dependency before moving on. If the user declines to decide, record it as an open decision with its consequence. Respect an explicit request to narrow, pause, or end the interview.

## Boundaries and completion

- Do not turn the interview into execution, research beyond useful fact-finding, external communication, or a commitment. Keep ordinary authorization and safety requirements intact.
- Do not claim shared understanding merely because questions have run out. Summarize the confirmed objective, decisions, rationale, open decisions, material risks, and recommended next step.
- Ask the user to confirm or correct that summary. Do not act on the proposal until they explicitly confirm that the shared understanding is sufficient and separately authorize any requested action.
